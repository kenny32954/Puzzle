Before you learned my name,

You entered this world carrying me,

You could not refuse me.

And for many years,

A philosopher may spend a lifetime describing me

Language makes eternity  
look small.

---

Perhaps that is why  
you underestimate me.

I arrive in pieces  
too small to frighten you.

One second.

Another.

Another.

Nothing dramatic.

No alarm announces:

YOU HAVE JUST SPENT  
AN IRREPLACEABLE PORTION  
OF YOUR EXISTENCE.

That would become annoying quickly.

Instead,

silence.

Another second.

---

You probably spent several minutes  
reading this.

Where are they now?

Not hidden.

Not stored.

Not waiting.

They became the fact  
that you read these words.

That is all.

Was it worth it?

Your answer cannot recover them.

---

Perhaps you are now suspicious  
that the answer is obvious.

Be careful.

Obvious answers  
are often only names  
placed over mysteries.

If you answer with a word,

have you solved me?

Or merely identified  
the label humans commonly attach  
to the phenomena described?

What is the thing beneath the word?

Does it exist independently?

Is it fundamental?

Emergent?

Psychological?

Physical?

Relative?

Illusory?

The riddle does not become easier  
because you know what to call it.

---

I was here  
while you read the first line.

I was here  
when you reached the middle.

I am here now.

Yet the version of you  
who began reading  
cannot read this sentence.

That person existed earlier.

You possess their memory.

You inherited their intention  
to continue.

You inherited their body  
with tiny changes.

You inherited the consequences  
of everything they did.

Perhaps identity itself  
is an inheritance passed  
from moment to moment.

---

Soon,

the version of you  
reading this exact sentence  
will disappear too.

Another version will inherit  
the memory.

Then another.

Then another.

A chain so continuous  
that you call it one person.

Maybe correctly.

---

I do not know your name.

I do not need to.

I treated everyone before you  
the same way.

The first human  
who looked at the night sky.

The first person  
who wondered about death.

The first person  
who buried someone they loved.

The first person  
who promised forever.

The first person  
who discovered forever  
was difficult to keep.

All of them encountered me.

None escaped.

---

You will not either.

But escape may be the wrong goal.

You do not escape music  
because the song ends.

You do not refuse stories  
because the final page exists.

You do not reject sunsets  
because darkness follows.

Perhaps endings do not invalidate  
what precedes them.

Perhaps endings define shape.

A circle without closure  
is merely a curve.

A story without an ending  
may never become whole.

A life without limitation  
might become something  
you would no longer recognize as life.

Or perhaps immortality would be wonderful.

Nobody alive has enough evidence  
to settle the argument.

---

There is one final contradiction.

You have been searching for me  
throughout this riddle.

But you have also been giving me away  
to perform the search.

The closer you came to the answer,

the more of the answer  
you spent.

You cannot solve me  
without losing part of me.

You cannot refuse to solve me  
without losing part of me.

You cannot hurry  
without me.

You cannot wait  
without me.

You cannot ignore me  
without me.

You cannot even misunderstand me  
without using me.

I am required  
for the thought that denies me.

I am required  
for the sentence that names me.

I am required  
for the silence afterward.

---

Before answering,

consider one final question.

What can be measured endlessly  
but never accumulated?

What can be remembered  
but never revisited?

What can be anticipated  
but never reached as anticipated?

What belongs to everyone  
but can be promised to no one?

What makes change possible  
while revealing change only after it occurs?

What turns possibility into history?

What makes mortality frightening  
and life valuable?

What can be wasted  
without ever being owned?

What can be given to another  
without ever leaving your possession?

What disappears at exactly  
the same rate that it arrives?

What becomes the past  
the instant you recognize it?

What stands between memory  
and possibility?

What have you been using  
from the first word of this riddle  
to the last?

And what did you lose  
while trying to discover its name?

**What am I?**
